# Project Overview

## Project Context

The Residential Construction Project was developed as a project-management case study for **UTS 49002 – Managing Projects**.

The project involved planning the delivery of a four-bedroom retirement home for **Peter and Jade Freestone in Forster, New South Wales**.

The project required the team to develop an integrated project-management approach covering the project's scope, schedule, resources, costs, risks, quality requirements, stakeholders, communications, and handover.

---

## Project Objective

The primary objective was to develop a structured project plan for the successful delivery of the proposed residential construction project.

The project plan was designed to establish:

* Clearly defined project objectives
* A controlled project scope
* Defined project deliverables
* A structured project schedule
* Identified project dependencies
* Appropriate resource allocation
* Project cost considerations
* Risk identification and treatment
* Quality requirements
* Stakeholder and communication arrangements
* A controlled approach to project delivery and handover

---

## Project Scope

The project scope covered the planning and management of the residential construction project from initial site preparation through to completion and handover.

### Major Project Phases

The project was structured around the following major phases:

1. **Site Preparation**
2. **Construction**
3. **External Works**
4. **Final Inspection and Handover**

These phases provided the high-level structure for planning project activities and dependencies.

---

## Key Deliverables

The project-management plan incorporated a range of interconnected deliverables, including:

* Project Charter
* Project Scope
* Work Breakdown Structure
* Project Task List and Task Dictionary
* Network Diagram
* Gantt Chart
* Risk Management Plan
* Resource and Cost Management Plan
* Quality Management activities
* Stakeholder Management
* Communication Management
* Project Governance
* Final Project Report

---

## Project Constraints and Considerations

The project required consideration of several factors that could affect successful delivery.

These included:

### Time

Construction activities needed to be sequenced appropriately to support the planned project completion date.

### Cost

Resources and project activities needed to be considered within the project's budget framework.

### Quality

Construction and project documentation needed to meet defined quality expectations.

### Resources

The project required appropriate allocation of personnel and other resources across project activities.

### Risk

Potential project risks needed to be identified, assessed, treated, and monitored.

### Stakeholders

The project involved multiple stakeholders whose requirements and communication needs needed to be considered throughout delivery.

## Project Lifecycle

The project-management approach connected planning activities across the project lifecycle:

```text
Initiation
    ↓
Scope Definition
    ↓
Project Planning
    ↓
Scheduling
    ↓
Risk & Resource Planning
    ↓
Quality Management
    ↓
Project Delivery
    ↓
Inspection & Handover
```

This structure helped demonstrate how individual project-management processes contribute to overall project delivery.

---

## Project Management Integration

One of the key characteristics of the project was the interdependence between project-management areas.

For example:

**Scope → Schedule → Resources → Cost → Risk → Quality → Delivery**

Changes to the project scope could affect activities and durations, which could subsequently affect resources, costs, risks, and the overall delivery plan.

The project therefore required the team to consider project-management decisions as interconnected rather than as isolated activities.

---

## Project Governance

The team established defined responsibilities for different project-management areas.

Responsibilities were distributed across team members, with individual roles supporting areas such as:

* Quality Assurance
* Scheduling
* Risk Management
* Scope and Work Breakdown Structure
* Resource and Cost Management

This division of responsibility supported accountability while requiring coordination between team members.

---

## Project Outcome

The project resulted in an integrated project-management plan for the proposed residential construction project.

The completed work demonstrated the application of project-management concepts to a realistic engineering and construction scenario, including planning, scheduling, risk management, resource planning, quality assurance, stakeholder management, and project documentation.
