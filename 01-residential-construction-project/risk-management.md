# Risk Management

## Overview

Risk management was an integral part of the Residential Construction Project management plan.

The team used a structured approach to identify, assess, treat, mitigate, and monitor risks that could affect project delivery.

The risk-management process was linked with other project-management activities, including scope, scheduling, resources, and project delivery.

---

## Risk Management Process

The project followed a structured risk-management approach:

```text
Risk Identification
        ↓
Risk Evaluation
        ↓
Risk Treatment
        ↓
Risk Mitigation
        ↓
Risk Monitoring
```

This provided a systematic way of considering potential threats to the project's objectives.

---

## Risk Identification

Potential risks were identified based on factors that could affect the project's:

* Schedule
* Cost
* Quality
* Resources
* Construction activities
* Project delivery
* Stakeholder requirements

Identifying risks during planning allowed the team to consider appropriate responses before risks could significantly affect project outcomes.

---

## Risk Evaluation

Identified risks were evaluated to understand their potential effect on the project.

The risk-management documentation considered the likelihood and impact of risks to support prioritisation.

This allowed the team to focus attention on risks that could have a greater effect on project objectives.

---

## Risk Treatment and Mitigation

Following evaluation, appropriate treatment and mitigation strategies were considered.

The purpose of mitigation was to reduce either:

* The likelihood of a risk occurring
* The potential impact if the risk occurred
* Or both

Risk responses were considered in the context of the project's activities, resources, schedule, and delivery requirements.

---

## Risk Monitoring

Risk management was treated as an ongoing activity rather than a one-time planning exercise.

The team maintained and updated the risk register throughout the project.

Monitoring allowed identified risks and their responses to remain visible as the project developed.

---

## Relationship with the Project Schedule

Risk management was connected to project scheduling.

For example, a risk affecting a construction activity could potentially influence:

```text
Risk
 ↓
Activity
 ↓
Duration
 ↓
Schedule
 ↓
Project Completion
```

This demonstrates why risk management needs to be considered alongside scheduling rather than separately from it.

---

## Relationship with Resources and Cost

Project risks could also influence resource and cost requirements.

A risk that caused additional work, delays, or changes to project activities could require additional resources or affect planned costs.

The project therefore considered risk as part of the wider project-management system.

## Risk Management Lessons

The project reinforced several important principles:

### 1. Identify Risks Early

Early identification provides more opportunity to develop appropriate responses.

### 2. Prioritise Risks

Not every risk has the same potential effect. Evaluation helps focus attention on higher-priority risks.

### 3. Monitor Risks Continuously

Risk conditions can change as a project progresses, making ongoing monitoring important.

### 4. Integrate Risk with Other PM Areas

Risk decisions can affect scope, schedule, resources, cost, and quality.
