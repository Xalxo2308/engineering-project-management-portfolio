# Stakeholder & Communication Management Analysis

## Overview

Effective stakeholder and communication management was an important component of the Residential Construction Project. The project documentation established stakeholder identification, engagement strategies, communication responsibilities, reporting arrangements, and mechanisms for maintaining project traceability.

The project distinguished between stakeholder engagement and day-to-day project communication. Stakeholders were analysed according to their role, influence, and level of interest, while the communication plan defined how project information would be shared.

---

## Stakeholder Management

The project identified stakeholders based on their potential influence on project delivery and their interest in the outcome.

Key stakeholders included:

- Project Sponsor / Homeowners – Peter & Jade Freestone
- Project Manager
- Mid Coast Council
- Building Construction Contractor
- Design Consultant
- Academic Advisor
- Building inspectors
- Subcontractors
- Suppliers

The stakeholder management plan aimed to maintain stakeholder alignment, address concerns, support regulatory compliance, and reduce risks associated with unmet expectations or poor communication.

### Stakeholder Engagement Approach

Different stakeholders required different levels and methods of engagement.

| Stakeholder Group | Primary Need | Engagement Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Project Sponsor / Homeowners | Progress, decisions and approvals | Regular updates and strategic decision-making |
| Project Manager | Coordination and reporting | Lead communication and stakeholder engagement |
| Council / Regulatory Authorities | Compliance and approvals | Formal correspondence, submissions and inspections |
| Contractors | Construction coordination | Regular site meetings and issue resolution |
| Design Consultants | Design coordination | Design consultations and project updates |
| Academic Supervisor | Project oversight | Weekly status reporting |
| Suppliers | Materials and procurement coordination | Procurement and schedule communication |

The project plan included bi-weekly oversight meetings with the Sponsor and Project Manager, weekly project status reports, formal Council submissions and inspections, site meetings with contractors and subcontractors, and stakeholder engagement around major milestones.

## Communication Management

The formal Communication Plan was designed to ensure that project information was timely, relevant and accurate.

Its main objectives were to:

1. Provide consistent updates on project progress, risks and changes.
2. Enable two-way communication and stakeholder feedback.
3. Maintain documentation for accountability and project traceability.

The planned communication matrix included:

| Communication | Audience | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project Kick-off Meeting | Project stakeholders | Once | Project overview, objectives and roles |
| Oversight Meeting | Sponsor and Project Manager | Bi-weekly | Progress, issues and change decisions |
| Status Reports | Sponsor and Academic Supervisor | Weekly | Milestones, risks and upcoming activities |
| Site Meetings | Contractors and subcontractors | Daily/weekly | Site coordination and issue resolution |
| Council Communications | Regulatory stakeholders | As required | Compliance and inspection coordination |

## Communication Risks

Communication itself was recognised as an area requiring active management.

The team's individual assessment identified:

- Delayed responses
- Communication challenges under pressure
- Potential task overlap
- Miscommunication

Mitigation approaches included:

- Scheduled meetings
- Agreed communication protocols
- Clearer task allocation
- Regular status check-ins
- Sharing meeting minutes
- Early peer reviews of major deliverables

These controls demonstrate that communication was treated not only as an administrative activity but also as part of project risk management. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

## Key Learning

One of the main lessons from the project was that communication needs both **structure and flexibility**.

Structured meetings and documented action items improve accountability and traceability. At the same time, informal communication channels such as group messaging can support rapid coordination when deadlines approach.

The project experience also demonstrated a potential weakness of informal communication: action points can be missed when discussions occur verbally or through rapid chat exchanges without sufficient documentation.

A stronger future approach would therefore combine:

**Structured meetings → documented decisions → assigned actions → progress tracking → follow-up review**
